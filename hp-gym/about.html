Two racks,
eleven members

That is what HP Gym was in 2016 — a shed off Trichy Road with second-hand plates and a coach who had quit a corporate job to do this properly. Nine years later the floor is twelve thousand square feet, and the method has not changed at all.

2016 The year the first two racks went in
Our story

Built by people
who train here

Vikram Selvan started HP Gym after a decade of watching commercial chains sell annual contracts to people who never came back in March. His theory was simple and slightly unfashionable: if a gym actually coaches its members, the members stay, and nobody has to be sold anything twice.

So there is no sales floor here, no commission on memberships, and no supplement counter by the door. What we spent that money on instead is coaches — fourteen of them for twelve hundred members, which is roughly three times the ratio of the chain down the road.

The rest went into equipment that survives being used hard: four power racks, two competition platforms, calibrated plates, and dumbbells that go to sixty kilos because somebody eventually needs them to.

What we believe

Four rules we
do not bend

They sound obvious written down. Most gyms break at least three of them.

01

Coach first, sell never

Nobody here earns commission on a membership. If a plan is wrong for you, the coach will say so out loud.

02

Progress gets written down

If it is not in your log it did not happen. Every session, every load, reviewed monthly by a human being.

03

Slow beats spectacular

We will not put you on a crash diet or a 5 am bootcamp you cannot sustain. Boring, repeatable, forever.

04

Everyone gets the same floor

No members-only corner, no VIP hours. The 62-year-old and the state lifter share the rack.

The floor

Twelve thousand
square feet

Split across four zones so the deadlift platform and the 6 am conditioning class never fight over the same corner.

What's in the building

  • Strength zone. Four power racks, two competition platforms, calibrated plates, a full set of specialty bars.
  • Free weights. Dumbbells from 2 to 60 kg in 2 kg steps, twelve benches, cable stacks on both walls.
  • Conditioning turf. An 18-metre strip for sleds, ropes and carries, plus the bag rack for boxing.
  • Recovery room. Mats, bands, foam rollers and a physio table Suresh actually uses.

And the boring essentials

  • Clean changing rooms. Six showers, hot water from 5 am, checked twice a day.
  • Air handling. Cross-ventilated with industrial fans — it is Coimbatore, this matters.
  • Parking. Free covered two-wheeler parking, twelve car spaces at the rear.
  • Water and ice. Free RO water and an ice machine, because somebody always needs one.
The team

Fourteen coaches,
one standard

Every coach here holds a recognised certification, shadows a senior coach for six paid weeks before taking their own members, and does two workshops a year on the gym's money. They also all train on this floor — which is the part that actually keeps them honest.
