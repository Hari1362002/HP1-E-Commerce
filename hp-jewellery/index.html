Anatomy of a
solitaire

Six parts, and every one of them changes the price. We label all of them on the tag, so you can tell what you are paying for the gold, the stone and the hand that set it.

Table facet

The flat crown of the stone. Cut too shallow and light escapes out the back instead of returning to your eye.

Girdle

The waist where crown meets pavilion. Ours are laser-inscribed with the certificate number.

Four-prong basket

Platinum-tipped so the claws hold their shape for decades, not months.

22K / 916 gold

91.6% fine gold, stamped by a BIS assaying centre. The hallmark is on the inner shank.

Comfort-fit shank

Domed on the inside so it sits over the knuckle without pinching in Coimbatore heat.

Hand polish

Nine stages of finishing, all done at the bench. No tumbling, no shortcuts.

Made by hand

Six weeks at
the bench

Nothing here is cast in bulk and drop-shipped. A bridal haram passes through eleven pairs of hands on Oppanakara Street before it reaches its box — wax, cast, file, set, polish, assay, and then polish again.

  1. 01

    You see the wax first

    Every bespoke commission starts with a 3D print you can actually try on, so the proportions are settled before a gram of gold is melted.

  2. 02

    The invoice is itemised

    Metal weight, stone weight, wastage and making charges appear as separate lines. No rolled-up "per gram" figure hiding the margin.

  3. 03

    It comes back for life

    Free cleaning, re-polishing and prong checks, forever. Exchange at the day's full gold rate, minus nothing.
